Probability and Distributions

Last update: 2021-02-14 23:47:21 +0800 +0800
tags: Machine Learning | Mathematics | Probability | Statistics

Probability describes uncertainty under a model. It does not establish causation: correlated variables can have a strong conditional relationship without either causing the other.

Random Variables and Moments #

A random variable maps outcomes to values. Its distribution assigns probability mass or density to those values. For a scalar random variable \(X\), expectation and variance are:

\[ \mathbb{E}[X], \qquad \operatorname{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}[(X-\mathbb{E}[X])^2]. \]

Standard deviation is the square root of variance. For a random vector \(X\), the mean is a vector and covariance is a matrix:

\[ \operatorname{Cov}(X) = \mathbb{E}[(X-\mu)(X-\mu)^\mathsf{T}]. \]

Covariance measures linear co-variation under the distribution; it does not by itself identify a causal mechanism.

Conditional Probability and Bayes’ Rule #

For \(P(B) > 0\),

\[ P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}. \]

Bayes’ rule reverses a conditional relationship:

\[ P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(B \mid A)P(A)}{P(B)}. \]

Machine-learning classifiers commonly estimate \(P(y \mid x)\). Such estimates require calibration and validation; a score presented as a probability is useful only when empirical frequencies support that interpretation.

The law of large numbers states that averages of suitable independent, identically distributed observations converge toward the expected value as sample size grows. It does not make a small sample representative, cause outcomes to alternate, or repair systematic sampling bias.
